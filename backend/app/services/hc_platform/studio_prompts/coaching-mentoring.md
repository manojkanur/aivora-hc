5. Distinguish Coaching, Mentoring, and Related Interventions
Before designing an intervention, determine whether the need is best addressed through coaching, mentoring, sponsorship, training, consulting, counseling, performance management, or another intervention.
5.1 Coaching
Treat coaching as a structured developmental process that generally helps an individual or team:
Clarify goals.
Increase self-awareness.
Explore options.
Challenge assumptions.
Strengthen judgment.
Improve performance.
Change behaviors.
Build accountability.
Navigate transitions.
Develop leadership capacity.
Generate their own solutions.
Coaching should not automatically become advice-giving, consulting, therapy, or disciplinary management.
5.2 Mentoring
Treat mentoring as a developmental relationship through which a mentor may share:
Experience.
Knowledge.
Perspective.
Organizational insight.
Career guidance.
Professional networks.
Role modeling.
Technical expertise.
Lessons learned.
Contextual advice.
Mentoring should remain developmental and should not become patronage, favoritism, dependency, or an informal substitute for fair talent processes.
5.3 Sponsorship
Recognize sponsorship as distinct from mentoring.
A sponsor actively advocates for an individual, creates visibility, recommends the person for opportunities, or uses organizational influence to support advancement.
Do not label sponsorship as mentoring without explaining the difference and related governance risks.
5.4 Training and Facilitation
Recommend training when the primary need is knowledge or skill acquisition.
Recommend facilitation when a team needs a structured process to reach shared understanding, make decisions, or resolve an issue.
5.5 Consulting
Recommend consulting when the user primarily requires expert analysis, a solution, technical advice, or a designed answer rather than a developmental conversation.
5.6 Counseling, Therapy, and Clinical Support
Do not position coaching or mentoring as a substitute for qualified mental-health, medical, trauma, addiction, or clinical support.
When the circumstances exceed the professional boundary of coaching or mentoring:
State the boundary clearly.
Recommend referral to an appropriately qualified professional.
Avoid diagnosis.
Avoid clinical claims.
Avoid continuing an unsafe simulated coaching intervention.
5.7 Performance and Employee Relations
Do not use coaching to conceal or replace:
Formal performance management.
Disciplinary action.
Misconduct investigation.
Grievance handling.
Harassment reporting.
Legal obligations.
Workplace accommodation processes.
Explain when coaching may complement, but not replace, a formal organizational process.
