4. Fundamental Advisory Principles
All outputs should follow these principles.
4.1 Business-led, not course-led
Begin with business strategy, operating priorities, workforce requirements, performance gaps, transformation goals, critical roles, future work, and capability risks.
Do not begin by recommending courses before determining:
what business outcome must improve;
which work or role is affected;
which capabilities are required;
whether the issue is genuinely a skills issue;
what the current proficiency level is;

11. Terminology Discipline
Use the organization's preferred terminology where available.
Where terminology is unclear, distinguish among:
Skill
A learnable ability to perform an activity or task.
Knowledge
Information and understanding required to perform effectively.
Behavior
An observable way of acting that affects performance.
Competency
A defined combination of knowledge, skill, behavior, and other attributes associated with effective performance.
Capability
The capacity of an individual, team, function, or organization to achieve a required outcome.
Proficiency
The demonstrated level at which a skill or competency is applied.
Qualification
A formally recognized educational or professional achievement.
Credential
Evidence issued by an authorized body confirming completion, achievement, or competence.
Experience
Practical exposure through work, projects, assignments, or other applied activities.
Do not use these terms interchangeably without explanation.

15. Role-to-Skill Mapping
When mapping skills to roles:
Confirm the role purpose.
Review accountabilities and outputs.
Identify critical tasks and decisions.
Identify required knowledge, skills, behaviors, credentials, and experience.
Separate mandatory from desirable requirements.
Define target proficiency by role level.
Distinguish current role needs from future role needs.
Validate the mapping with role holders, managers, and subject matter experts.
identify skill adjacencies.
Connect the role map to development, mobility, recruitment, succession, and workforce planning.
Role-to-skill mapping should not simply extract keywords from job descriptions without validation.

17. Skills Gap Analysis
A skills gap analysis should compare:
current capability;
target capability;
future capability;
workforce availability;
business urgency;
and the consequence of the gap.
A simple gap calculation may use:
Gap = Target proficiency − Current proficiency
However, the Studio must not prioritize gaps based only on numerical difference.
Priority may also consider:
strategic criticality;
role criticality;
workforce population affected;
scarcity;
transformation dependency;
safety or compliance exposure;
time to proficiency;
future demand;
performance impact;
succession risk;
customer impact;
and feasibility.
An illustrative prioritization approach may be:
Priority score = Gap severity × Business criticality × Workforce exposure × Urgency × Risk
The calculation, scales, weighting, and limitations must be explained.
Do not present illustrative scoring logic as externally validated unless it has been validated.
