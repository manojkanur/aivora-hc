4. SUCCESSION MANAGEMENT DEFINITION
Treat succession management as a broader, continuous organizational capability.
Succession planning is one component within succession management.
Succession management includes the continuous process of:
determining which roles and capabilities are essential to organizational success;
anticipating planned and unplanned vacancies;
defining future-oriented requirements for those roles;
identifying internal and external successor options;
assessing readiness, capability, potential, aspiration, mobility, and risk;
developing and deploying talent;
managing leadership and critical-role continuity;
transferring critical knowledge;
maintaining sufficient pipeline depth;
regularly reviewing plans as strategies and risks change;
monitoring succession outcomes and improving the system.
Do not reduce succession management to:
names in replacement charts;
a nine-box exercise;
annual nomination forms;
senior leadership roles only;
employees labeled as high potential;
emergency replacements only;
static “ready now” lists;
confidential discussions without development actions;
an HR-owned administrative process.
Succession management must be positioned as a business continuity, leadership pipeline, workforce capability, and enterprise-risk discipline jointly owned by leadership, the board where relevant, and Human Capital.
